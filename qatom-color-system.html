Brand guideline · Color system

Qatom color

Make verdigris the brand,
not the accent.

Black and white is a utility, not a brand. Color equity is the cheapest memorability Qatom can buy, and it is built one way: by making verdigris the field that carries the mark, shown constantly, everywhere it counts.

The principle

Own the field, not a detail on it.

Brands own a color when the color is the surface, not a small mark sitting on a neutral. A viewer encodes one memory unit: the verdigris field with the orbital mark. That is the Tiffany box, the Cadbury wrapper, the UPS truck. It reads at twenty four pixels, in a crowded feed, before anyone reads the name. A colored logo on a white card does not do this. A field of color does.

Distinctiveness

The orbital, slashed circle is already unusual. A distinctive shape plus one specific color, locked together, is what becomes ownable.

Repetition

Recognition is built by frequency. The color has to appear on the touchpoints that fire thousands of times, not the hero image shipped twice a year.

The hierarchy

One hero. One signature. One utility.

Every color pairing has a job. Lead with the field. Reach for the others only where the field cannot go.

Hero · use first, use most

White on verdigris

The field. This is the brand at full strength. Default for every profile avatar, app icon, favicon background, and any surface small enough to be recognized by color alone.

Signature · dark contexts

Verdigris on near-black

The second voice. Premium and technical, matched to the website grounds. For banners, video, dark decks, and product UI.

Utility · function only

Black and white

Legibility and reproduction. Print, tiny favicons, embossing, one color partner lockups, anywhere color cannot render true. Never the default face.

How to make it integral

Five moves, in order of impact.

  1. Lead with the field on every avatar. Set LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube, and Slack to white on verdigris now. Four identical verdigris discs across four platforms is the fastest color memory available.
  2. Lock one hex. Use a single verdigris everywhere, with no drift. Inconsistent teals are why most companies never own their color.
  3. Put color on the highest frequency touchpoints. Avatar, favicon, primary button, link color, email signature chip, slide master accent, loading state. That is where equity compounds.
  4. Name it and give it a reason. Verdigris is the patina copper and bronze earn as they oxidize, proof that something is real and has held up over time. It ties straight to integrity built into every asset.
  5. Keep everything else quiet. The moment verdigris becomes one of five equal colors it stops being ownable. Oxide and pine are supporting grounds, never co-stars.

Do

  • Default to the verdigris field for avatars and app icons.
  • Use the exact locked hex, every time.
  • Let verdigris be the one loud thing in an otherwise neutral, cool system.
  • Pair the color with the orbital mark so shape and color are learned together.

Don't

  • Lead with the black and white duo out of habit. It builds no color equity.
  • Set body text in white on verdigris. It is logo strength contrast, not text strength.
  • Drift the teal between assets, or add rival accent colors.
  • Reserve the color for rare hero moments while everyday touchpoints stay mono.

The name

Why verdigris.

Verdigris is the green blue patina that copper and bronze earn as they oxidize. It is the color of a surface that has been exposed, tested, and proven over time. That is the argument Qatom makes about every asset it settles: real, verifiable, and built to hold up. A color with a reason gets used consistently, because the team believes in it. That belief is what keeps the hex from drifting, and consistency is what turns a color into a brand.
